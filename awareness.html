Cybersecurity Tips and Guides

Cybersecurity means protecting devices, accounts, and information. Everyday habits help reduce online risks.

Everyday examples

  • Installing an app update or checking a message before opening it.
  • Sharing photos without revealing your address or school schedule.

Build safer habits

  • Enable automatic updates and install apps from trusted sources.
  • Avoid unexpected links and attachments; verify through a known contact or official app.
  • Review privacy settings and share only necessary personal details.
  • Back up important schoolwork and files.

Learn more: CISA — 4 Easy Ways to Stay Safe Online (PDF).

02

Online Scams

Online scams use false promises or impersonation to take your money or personal information.

Examples & warning signs

  • Fake giveaways that require a payment to claim a prize.
  • Fraudulent shops with unusually low prices and no reliable contact information.
  • Unexpected messages demanding urgent payment or secrecy.

Protect yourself

  • Check a seller’s reputation using independent sources.
  • Pause before paying; verify requests through a trusted channel.
  • Do not share passwords or verification codes with a sender.
  • Report suspicious accounts to the platform. If money was sent, contact your payment provider promptly.

Learn more: Federal Trade Commission (FTC) — How To Avoid a Scam.

03

Phishing Awareness

Phishing is an attempt to trick you into sharing information or downloading harmful software through fake emails, messages, or websites.

Fictional example — do not follow this request

“Your StudyCloud account closes in 10 minutes! Send your password and verification code to support@studycloud-help.example to keep access.”

This is an invented service and address for learning only.

Spot the warning signs

  • An urgent threat pressures you to act without checking.
  • The sender asks for a password and a private verification code.
  • A familiar display name or polished writing does not prove a message is genuine.

Respond safely

  • Do not reply, open attachments, or follow links in a suspicious message.
  • Open the official app or type the known website address yourself.
  • Report the message as phishing, then delete it.
  • If you shared a password, change it through the real service and secure the account.

Learn more: Federal Trade Commission (FTC) — How To Recognize and Avoid Phishing Scams.

04

Password Security

A strong password is long and unique. Using different passwords helps stop one stolen password from exposing other accounts.

Examples & risky habits

  • Use a password manager to generate a long, random password. NIST recommends at least 15 characters.
  • Short passwords, names, birthdays, and reused passwords are risky.
  • Unexpected sign-in alerts may mean someone is trying to access your account.

Protect your accounts

  • Give every account its own password. Where available, consider a passkey, which uses a digital key instead of a password and resists phishing.
  • Use a reputable password manager to generate and store passwords.
  • Turn on multifactor authentication (MFA), which adds another verification step.
  • Never share passwords or one-time codes, and reject sign-in prompts you did not initiate.

Learn more: National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) — How Do I Create a Good Password?.

05

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying is bullying through digital devices and platforms. Repeated harassment can affect someone’s confidence, concentration, and emotional well-being.

Examples & warning signs

  • Repeated hurtful messages, rumors, or humiliating posts.
  • Fake profiles used to impersonate or embarrass someone.
  • Someone feeling anxious about messages or withdrawing after online harassment.

Take safe action

  • Save evidence, such as screenshots and dates, without spreading harmful content.
  • Block and report abusive accounts using platform tools.
  • Talk to a trusted adult, teacher, counselor, or supportive person.
  • Support the person targeted. Avoid retaliation or sharing the abuse. If there is immediate danger, seek emergency help.

Learn more: StopBullying.gov — What Is Cyberbullying.

06

Identity Theft

Identity theft happens when someone uses your personal information without permission to impersonate you or commit fraud.

Examples & warning signs

  • A stolen ID photo or leaked account details used to open an account in your name.
  • Unfamiliar transactions, unexpected account changes, or messages about accounts you did not create.

Protect your information

  • Limit public details such as your birth date, address, and ID numbers.
  • Share sensitive information only with verified services when necessary.
  • Review account activity and enable sign-in notifications and MFA.
  • If you suspect misuse, secure affected accounts, contact the relevant provider, and report to your local authorities. IdentityTheft.gov provides U.S. reporting and recovery guidance; reporting procedures vary by country.

Learn more: Federal Trade Commission (FTC) — IdentityTheft.gov.
